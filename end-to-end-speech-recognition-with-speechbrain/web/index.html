Listen to the tone, name the digit.

A digit recognizer built on synthetic speech-like audio: every word is synthesized as a pair of formants with its own length, turned into log-band cepstra, and classified by a random forest that also reports how easily it confuses the digits with each other.

Architecture

How it works

Every digit has two formant frequencies and a duration, so a word is a short envelope over three sine waves with a little noise on top. The signal is cut into twenty frames, the spectrum of every frame is pooled into twelve log bands, and a discrete cosine transform of the log bands gives the cepstral vector the classifier reads. A random forest is fitted on seventy percent of the generated words, and the rest is used for the accuracy and for the recall of every digit.
