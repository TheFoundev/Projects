Two networks argue until the shapes look real.

A tiny generative adversarial network on 16 by 16 shape images: a generator turns a vector of noise into circles and rectangles, a discriminator tells the fakes from the real ones, and the two are trained against each other until the sample grid looks like the dataset.

Architecture

How it works

The dataset is 160 synthetic images of 16 by 16 pixels: a circle or a rectangle in white on black, scaled to the range from minus one to one. The generator is a dense block followed by two transposed convolutions that turn a vector of twelve numbers into a picture; the discriminator is two strided convolutions and a sigmoid. Each step trains the discriminator on real and on generated images and then the generator against it, and the run ends with a grid of eight samples that is written to the artefacts.

The API

Endpoints

MethodPathWhat it does
GET/api/summaryThe two losses of the last training, the number of steps and the size of the dataset
GET/api/samplesThe sample grid that the last training wrote
GET/api/gridThe sample grid as a picture, read from the artefacts of the last training
POST/api/generateDraw a new sample from the generator and answer with the picture
POST/api/trainTrain the pair again and answer with the new losses
GET/console.htmlThe console of this site
