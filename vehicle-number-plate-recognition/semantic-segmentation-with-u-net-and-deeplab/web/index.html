A picture in, the shape of the thing in it out.

U-Net trains a small convolutional network that takes a grey picture and paints the mask of the shapes in it, and it is scored with the dice coefficient and the share of the pixels it gets right.

What it does

The capabilities

Every panel below is served by the real API of this project — nothing here is a mock-up.

◆

A mask for every picture

The network paints a mask of the same size for every picture it is given.

▤

The way back up keeps the detail

The up sample is joined with the picture of the same size, so the edges stay sharp.

✓

A dice you can read

The dice coefficient says how much of the shape the mask and the truth have in common.

◑

The pixels counted as well

The pixel accuracy says how many of the pixels are on the right side.

⛨

A test set that was not trained on

The score is measured on the pictures the network did not see.

↻

A new training on demand

The console trains the network again and shows the metrics of the new run.

Architecture

How it works

The network has the shape of a U-Net: two convolution blocks, a down sample, a second pair, a second down sample, the bottleneck and the way back up with the up sample and the concatenation of the skip connection of the same size. It is compiled with the binary cross entropy and fitted with Adam on the training pictures. The score of the run is the dice coefficient of the prediction and the true mask, averaged over the test set, and the pixel accuracy is the share of the pixels that are on the right side of the half-way mark.
